FAQ

Common Questions

What is the $50 service fee for?

If we perform a full diagnostic and recovery attempt and are unable to recover any of your data, we charge a flat $50 service fee. This covers our time, equipment use, and the diagnostic work performed. The diagnostic itself is always free — you only pay the $50 if recovery fails. If we recover your data, you pay the quoted recovery price, not the service fee.

How long does data recovery take?

Logical recoveries (deleted files, corrupted file system) are often completed same day or next day. Mechanical hard drive recoveries that require parts take 3–7 business days typically. Phone recoveries vary by damage type. Rush service is available — ask when you contact us.

What are my chances of getting my data back?

It depends entirely on the type of failure and how the device was treated after the data loss. Logical failures (deleted, formatted) have high recovery rates if you stopped using the device immediately. Mechanical failures depend on the severity. We'll tell you honestly after the diagnostic what we expect to recover.

Do I need to bring my device in person?

Local customers in and around Kokomo, Indiana can drop off in person. If you're outside the area, we accept mail-in devices nationwide. We'll walk you through how to safely pack and ship your device.

Is my data kept private?

Absolutely. We recover your data, hand it back to you, and keep no copies. Your files are your files.

My phone got wet — what should I do right now?

Power it off immediately if it's still on. Do not try to charge it. Do not use a hair dryer. Put it in a bag of silica gel packets if you have them, or just leave it off and call us right away. Time matters with water damage.

Can you recover from a phone that won't turn on?

Often yes. A non-booting phone doesn't mean the data is gone — it usually means the logic board or software has failed while the storage chip is still intact. We can attempt chip-level recovery in many cases.

My hard drive is clicking — is it too late?

Stop using it immediately. Clicking usually means the read/write heads are damaged. Every spin of the drive risks further damage. Power it off and contact us — the sooner the better.
